The Project in 3 Plain Bullets
  1. The Money: The Indian Government gives each Member of Parliament (MP) ₹5 Crores every year to build drinking water plants, village roads, and school classrooms. The MP never receives cash—they only write a letter recommending what to build. The local District Collector hires a contractor, and the central treasury transfers the funds upon completion.
  2. The Problem: Over 120,000 projects happen across India at the same time, but district offices only have 2 or 3 engineers. Because human officers can only check about 1 out of 100 files, corrupt contractors take advantage by submitting fake bills, splitting large contracts into small ₹4.9 Lakh chunks to dodge open public bidding, or claiming buildings are finished when nothing was ever built.
  3. The Solution (SATARK): An automated software watchdog that cross-checks the "Work Diary" (where officers type what is completed) against the "Central Bank Passbook" (where real money leaves the treasury). In under 1 second, it flags fake claims, freezes unauthorized payouts, and locks GPS satellite photo evidence with an unalterable digital seal.
How It Works in Real Life: The "Ghost Building"
The Invisible Hospital Example

The Everyday Comparison: Imagine you pay a carpenter ₹50,000 for a bedroom wardrobe. Two weeks later, he hands you a stamped receipt saying "100% Finished" and takes your money. But when you open your bedroom door, there is only an empty floor—no wood, no wardrobe, nothing. The wardrobe exists only on paper.

What Happens in the Village: A contractor and a compromised local engineer sign a completion certificate for a ₹25 Lakh village health clinic. They collect public tax money from the treasury. But on the ground, there is only an empty field of grass and grazing cows. No foundation was ever dug.

How SATARK Catches It: SATARK compares the Work Diary with the Central Bank Account: "Hold on! You claim a whole hospital is 100% completed, but the bank shows you paid ₹0 for bricks, ₹0 for cement, and ₹0 for workers!" It instantly sounds an alarm, halts payment, and dispatches a verified inspection squad.
